About me

I am a biomedical researcher specialising in Ehlers–Danlos syndromes (EDS) and Hypermobility Spectrum Disorders (HSD). My research investigates how interactions between cells and the surrounding extracellular matrix influence connective-tissue function in EDS/HSD, with a particular focus on cell adhesion and cellular mechanobiology. I combine cell biology, mechanobiology and biophysical approaches to investigate the mechanisms underlying connective-tissue dysfunction.

My work has established a new mechanobiological research direction in EDS/HSD, proposing a stepwise pathomechanism linking altered cell–matrix interactions and fibroblast mechanics to connective-tissue dysfunction.

Following completion of my PhD and subsequent postdoctoral research at the University of Warwick, I am continuing to develop my research programme independently. I am currently preparing my experimental findings for publication and developing plans for the next stage of my research.
